The capital of India is **New Delhi**.

---

Here's an interesting myth about Delhi, often referred to as **"The Curse of Delhi"** or **"The Curse of the Builders of Delhi"**:

The myth suggests that any ruler who attempts to build a new city or capital in Delhi is destined for a short reign, a downfall, and their city will eventually be abandoned. Delhi has historically been a site of numerous cities being built and subsequently falling into ruin, leading to this fascinating superstition.

The most famous instance often cited to support this myth involves the **Sultan Ghiyas ud din Tughlaq** and the **Sufi Saint Nizamuddin Auliya** in the early 14th century.

1.  **The Conflict:** Sultan Tughlaq was building his grand new capital, **Tughlaqabad Fort**, to the south of present-day Delhi. At the same time, the highly revered Sufi saint, Nizamuddin Auliya, was constructing a *baoli* (stepwell) for the public in his spiritual center. The Sultan reportedly forbade his laborers from working on the *baoli* and even diverted oil supplies, leaving the saint's workers in the dark.
2.  **The Saint's Prophecies:** Angered by the Sultan's actions, Nizamuddin Auliya uttered two famous prophecies:
    *   **"Hunuz Dilli dur ast!"** (Delhi is still far away!) – This was in response to the Sultan, who was returning from a military campaign and had threatened the saint upon his arrival in Delhi.
    *   **"Ya toh Ujare, ya Guzar!"** (Either it will be deserted, or occupied by nomads/shepherds!) – This was about Tughlaqabad Fort itself.
3.  **The Fulfilling of the Curse:** As fate would have it, Sultan Ghiyas ud din Tughlaq never reached his new capital. He died in an accident (some say planned by his son) while resting in a temporary pavilion on the outskirts of Delhi before he could even properly enter Tughlaqabad. True to the second prophecy, Tughlaqabad was indeed soon deserted, remaining largely abandoned to this day.

This legend reinforced the belief that Delhi is a powerful, almost mystical land that resists anyone trying to fully conquer or establish a permanent reign without respecting its spiritual essence or its existing inhabitants. It adds a layer of mystery and spiritual warning to the city's long and tumultuous history of destruction and rebirth.
